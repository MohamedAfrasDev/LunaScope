## D-01 analysis — DEM selection (13 Nov 2026)

QuickMap and Moon Trek are portals; the numerical input must be a pinned file. Candidates are the LOLA south-polar gridded DEMs. Values below were read from the PDS label and HTTP headers on 13 Nov 2026; nothing has been downloaded yet.

| | A. `LDEM_80S_20MPP_ADJ.TIF` (**recommended primary**) | B. `LDEM_80S_20M.IMG` V2.0 (**cross-check**) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Publisher | NASA GSFC PGDA (Barker et al. 2023) | NASA PDS Geosciences (LOLA team, 2017) |
| Size | 2,696,082,051 B | 1,848,320,000 B |
| Format | cloud-optimized GeoTIFF (HTTP range reads work) | PDS3 label + 16-bit raw image |
| Grid | 20 m, polar stereographic, about ±304 km from pole | 30,400 × 30,400, 20 m, same extent |
| Frame | MOON_ME of DE421 (landing page) | "MEAN EARTH/POLAR AXIS OF DE421" (label) |
| Reference sphere | 1,737,400 m, planetocentric — **verified in the GeoTIFF CRS** (header read by range request) | 1,737.4 km, verified in label |
| Units | metres, float32 (range −7296.8 … 7027.2 m) | `HEIGHT = DN × 0.5 m` relative to the sphere (range −7297 … 7027 m) |
| Nodata | declared NaN; none in the inspected pole window; every pixel otherwise gets a value (natural-neighbor fill) | no missing constant in the label; gridded by interpolation |
| Registration | **pixel-is-area** (`AREA_OR_POINT=Area`); corner transform (−304000, 304000), 20 m | label says pixel registration |
| Uncertainty layers | **height error, effective resolution, spot counts** | counts product (`LDEC`) only |
| Track geolocation | adjusted; removes most geolocation-driven height errors | older solution; geolocation errors map to large height errors on slopes |
| Checksum | not published — compute after download | not published — compute after download |
| Terms | cite Barker et al. 2023 and data DOI; no explicit licence text found | PDS public archive |

**Why A as primary.** The horizon depends on slopes near the site, and the 2023 paper reports that uncorrected track geolocation errors become large vertical errors on slopes. A also ships height-error and effective-resolution layers, which are exactly the "DEM accuracy and resolution" inputs the uncertainty budget (spec 9.5) needs.

**Why B stays.** B has a complete PDS label (CRS-equivalent, sphere, units all verified) and is an independent release. Running the same site on A and B is a built-in DEM-source sensitivity check (FR-12).

**Consequences to carry into the code**
- **There is no nodata to detect.** Interpolated cells are indistinguishable from measured ones, so "nodata is unknown" (spec 8.4) will not flag sparse-coverage areas. D-01b below proposes a quality mask from effective resolution.
- **Coverage.** About ±304 km from the pole: the tile spans ±304 km, so a 150 km horizon radius is fully covered only for sites within about 154 km of the pole (roughly 85°S and poleward, at about 30.3 km per degree). Other sites need the 75°S products, so the footprint check must be enforced.
- **Sphere.** Both products use a 1,737.4 km sphere (A to be confirmed), so `reference_radius_km = 1737.4` and `ground_height_m` = DEM height need no datum conversion.
- **Pixel registration.** A is pixel-is-area, so sample at pixel centres (corner + 0.5 px). The two products share one grid and orientation: in a 64 × 64 window at the pole, A minus PDS (`DN × 0.5`) had mean 0.29 m, RMS 1.5 m, correlation 0.9997. That is one small window, not a full comparison, and a known-location test is still advisable.

**Verification done on 13 Nov (no full download):** GDAL range reads of the GeoTIFF header, one 256 × 256 window, and a 64 × 64 window from the PDS product. **Update 13 Nov (later): both DEMs are downloaded and pinned.**

Both DEMs were re-hashed independently after download. Neither publisher provides a checksum, so these are our own pins; they prove identity of the bytes, not scientific suitability.

**Full-extent comparison (every pixel, 924,160,000; ADJ minus PDS `DN x 0.5`):** mean -0.13 m, RMS 1.76 m, max 476 m; 0 NaN cells in ADJ. Within 150 km of the pole (the horizon footprint): RMS 1.3 to 1.6 m per ring, 6,214 pixels (0.0035%) differ by more than 50 m and 219 (0.00012%) by more than 100 m; the largest is 177 m at about 131 km from the pole. By ring: 0-25 km RMS 1.56 m; 25-50 km 1.33; 50-100 km 1.39; 100-150 km 1.54; 150-200 km 1.71; 200-305 km 1.85.

**Reading.** The two releases agree to about 1.5 m RMS, so for most terrain the choice does not matter. The rare large differences are where the older track geolocation differs on steep terrain. This does *not* tell us how much a horizon moves: that needs the same site run on both DEMs once `DemTerrain` exists (the DEM-source sensitivity check, FR-12).

**Use terms (checked 13 Nov).**
- PDS file: the PDS Geosciences FAQ states that data sets archived in the PDS are in the public domain and should be cited like published research; publisher "NASA Planetary Data System".
- PGDA file: the product page makes only a citation request (Barker et al. 2023, DOI 10.3847/PSJ/acf3e1, and data DOI 10.60903/gsfcpgda-lola-spole) and states no separate licence. It is treated as a NASA-produced open dataset with a citation request; PGDA has not confirmed this in writing.

**Same-site horizon on both DEMs (13 Nov, first real-terrain result; real_data_unverified).** Site -89.5 deg, 0 E (the Horizons fixture site), observer mast 2 m, ground height from each DEM (ADJ -721.96 m, PDS -721.85 m), 150 km radius, 0.5 deg azimuth step, `DemTerrain` bilinear on pixel centres.

| Quantity | Result |
| --- | --- |
| Skyline | -0.25 to 9.59 deg, mean 4.6 deg; all 720 sectors certified |
| Sun at the fixture times | elevation 0.42 deg at azimuth 153.5 deg; skyline there 9.42 deg (ADJ) / 9.42 deg (PDS), so the Sun is hidden by terrain although it is above the flat horizon |
| ADJ vs PDS skyline | max difference **0.45 deg** (azimuths 14-17 deg, obstacle only 0.2 km away), RMS 0.089 deg; 11% of azimuths differ by more than 0.1 deg, 26% by more than 0.01 deg |
| Radius 100 / 150 / 200 km | skyline unchanged (0.0000 deg): this site's skyline is set within 100 km |
| Azimuth step 0.5 -> 0.25 -> 0.1 deg | max skyline change 0.043 deg, then 0.017 deg: **both exceed the 0.01 deg decision band** |
| Time (this machine) | window load 3.3 s (ADJ) / 1.1 s (PDS, 937 MB in RAM); horizon 1.8 s at 0.5 deg, 3.5 s at 0.25 deg, 8.6 s at 0.1 deg, 2.4 s at 200 km |

**What this means.**
- The DEM release matters at the 0.1 to 0.5 deg level wherever the skyline is set by terrain within a few hundred metres. For a decision band of 0.01 deg this is the dominant uncertainty at this site, larger than anything in the geometry path (milli-arcseconds). Any pass/fail near a horizon crossing must be reported with this DEM sensitivity.
- A 0.5 deg azimuth step is too coarse for a 0.01 deg band at this site; 0.1 deg still moves the skyline by 0.017 deg. Finer steps, or a better interpolation of the skyline between rays, are needed before a result near the skyline can be called converged.
- The 20 s per-site horizon budget is met with a wide margin at default settings (measured, one site, warm file cache).
- This site is one arbitrary point inside deep terrain, not a candidate recommendation. Nothing here is validated.

**Still not done:** D-01b (use of effective-resolution/error layers), a multi-site version of the DEM-source comparison, science-owner sign-off.

**Measured agreement (recorded real Horizons responses, 2027-01-10 00:00-06:00 UTC, hourly):**

| Case | Max total on-sky residual |
| --- | --- |
| Sun, -89.5 deg, 0 m | 0.00002 arcsec (0.02 mas) |
| Sun, -45 deg / 120 E (Sun 37 deg up) | 0.00002 arcsec |

| Sun, exact pole -90 deg | 0.00002 arcsec |
| Earth, -89.5 deg | 0.0016 arcsec (1.6 mas) |
| Earth, -89.9 deg / 222.7 E, 1500 m ground | 0.0016 arcsec |

The Sun residuals sit at Horizons' 9-decimal output rounding. The Earth offset is constant (about 1.6 mas), the same at two different sites, and was not isolated. Solar light deflection was computed at 0.002 to 0.003 mas, so it is **not** the cause (an earlier guess said it was). The 1-minute series (361 rows each) agree the same way.

**What this does and does not show.**
- It shows the **geometry path and local-frame conventions** agree with an independent implementation: east/north/up, longitude direction, latitude sign, ground height, and the pole convention (our east-from-longitude rule matches Horizons at the exact pole; SPICE's own `azlcpo` cannot define a frame there). Deliberately wrong longitude direction or latitude sign are caught (residual > 1 deg).
- It does **not** validate the terrain horizon, the DEM, or any physical claim, and the evidence is one 6-hour window of one date. Both paths use the same DE-series planetary data, so they are not independent in the underlying ephemeris.
- 0.01 arcsec is a proposed tolerance, about 3,600 times smaller than the 0.01 deg decision band. Exceeding it raises `GEOMETRY_REFERENCE_MISMATCH` and blocks a verified export.

**Still open for D-04:** a longer or multi-date comparison; whether to isolate the 1.6 mas Earth offset; DE441 sensitivity; sign-off. The `TLIST` sub-minute fallback is not built.

**Reading.** The release choice moves the skyline by a median RMS of 0.076 deg and by up to 2.6 deg at one site, far more than epsilon (0.01 deg) and far more than the geometry path. Where the two releases differ in ground height by several metres (-89.5/120, -88/90, -86/250) the whole skyline shifts, because the observer height is taken from each release. The release choice is therefore a first-order uncertainty for any pass/fail near the skyline and must be reported with every real-data result (the `dem_release_sensitivity` card does this). It is **not** evidence that either release is right. Choosing the primary release on quality grounds (D-01) stands; this table says how much the choice matters.
